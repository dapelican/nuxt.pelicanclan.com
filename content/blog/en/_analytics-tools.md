As you can see, I only recommend privacy-friendly analytics tools. Compared to Google Analytics:

- They are not free, except for the latest incumbent Beam Analytics.
- They are simpler to use, except probably for Matomo, which has a more complex dashboard (also richer).
- They are respectful of the visitors' data.
- They merely impact the pageload time of websites thanks to their lightweight code snippets.

## Criteria to choose a tool

The previous tools provide almost the same metrics, with the exception of Matomo that also provides the journey of individual visitors. I can see 2 criteria to make a choice:

1. The design of the dashboard. Pick a tool that has a dashboard that you find well designed.
2. The price. Pick a tool that is within your budget.

Note that I personally use Plausible. When I chose Plausible, it was over Fathom, only for pricing reasons. I didn't know the other competitors at the time.

## What do analytics tool do?

Analytics tools provide information about the traffic of a website, thanks to snippet(s) of code that gets automatically executed when a page is loaded or when an action is performed by a visitor.

In my opinion, they provide 4 types of information.

### 1. How people get to a website

It's interesting in 2 cases:

1. To track the performance of sollicited incoming links (or backlinks) to your website. These are the links you expect to exist because you took action for them to exist. You paid for them, or convinced someone to create them.
2. To discover unsollicited incoming links to your websites. It provides opportunities to reach out to their originators.

### 2. The most visited pages (top pages)

Ideally, you want your best performing pages to link to other relevant pages of your website (internal links). This is not always possible, but if it is, you can be on your way to building a potential [content hub](https://ahrefs.com/blog/content-hub/?utm_source=pelicanclan.com&utm_medium=article){target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-primary"}.

### 3. Where your visitors are / what devices your visitors use

This piece of information is the most useful when you get unexpected results. In other words, an "exotic" location arrives at the top of the list, and it is a pleasant surprise. It is an opportunity for creating new content specifically for this audience.

### 4. The other numbers

These numbers include the total number of visits, the total number of pageviews, the number of pageviews per visit, the bounce rate and the visit duration. Any website owner wants all of these numbers to improve over time. If they do, it means that the actions you take are paying off: the content you create finds its audience. Otherwise, it can mean that something is off: it can be a content problem, or a technical problem with the website.

## Are analytics tools worth it?

Analytics tools are worth it as long as long the data they provide is significant enough.

This leads to the problem of ad blockers, which also block analytics scripts. As of now, we can safely presume that at least [40% of internet users use ad blockers](https://backlinko.com/ad-blockers-users?utm_source=pelicanclan.com&utm_medium=article){target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-primary"}.

For now, it is possible to [proxy analytics scripts](https://plausible.io/docs/proxy/introduction?utm_source=pelicanclan.com&utm_medium=article){target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-primary"} so that they aren't automatically blocked by ad blockers.. But ad blockers might get more agresssive We will see how things evolve.
