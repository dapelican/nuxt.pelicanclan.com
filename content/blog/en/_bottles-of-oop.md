## Chapter 1: rediscovering simplicity

Design decisions should be made on a benefits/costs basis.

Good code lies somewhere in the middle between 2 extremes: on one end, "concrete/easy to understand" code, on the other end, "abstract/easy to change" code.

How can we judge good code? A subjective but useful way is to answer to these 3 questions:

1. How difficult was it to write?
2. How hard is it to understand?
3. How expansive will it be to change?

The second question applies to every developer writing or reviewing code. Code easy to understand is code that reflects the problem it is solving, or the **problem's domain**. This concept is really interesting.

In the case of the "99 Bottles of beer" problem, the problem's domain can be the following:

1. How many verse variants are there?
2. What are the similaries and differences of the verses?
3. What is the rule to determine which verse comes next?
